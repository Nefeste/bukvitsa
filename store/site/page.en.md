Bukvitsa is a set of word puzzles in Russian — the game is for people who know the language.
A proverb with a missing word, a riddle, a proverb with an extra letter, the end of a
proverb, a proverb in shuffled parts, an old word from Dahl’s dictionary: the answer is built
from a row of letters or picked from four, in about a minute. The texts come from Vladimir
Dahl’s “Proverbs of the Russian People”; after each puzzle you see where it comes from and
what it means.

## Daily puzzle

One for everyone, every day, free and without ads. Share the result as text; a missed day
waits in the archive — nothing expires.

## Notebooks

Themed sets — work and idleness, home and family, speech and silence, winter and holidays —
solved in any order, offline. The first notebook is always free; the
rest open with Bukvitsa+.

## Calm and honest

- No timers, lives, energy or streaks.
- Hints are free and never sold: remove extra letters, reveal a letter, show the answer.
- Ads only between puzzles, never on the first day and never after the daily puzzle.
- Bukvitsa+ is a single purchase, not a subscription: no ads forever and all notebooks.
- No sign-up: the game has no server of its own; progress stays on the phone.

## Questions

### Where do the puzzles come from?

From public-domain editions: Dahl’s “Proverbs of the Russian People” (2nd ed., 1879) and
his “Explanatory Dictionary” (1880–1882).
Every puzzle in the game names its source.

### Is there an English version?

No. The puzzles are the Russian language itself — proverbs, riddles and old words — so
there is nothing to translate. The game is for Russian speakers anywhere.
